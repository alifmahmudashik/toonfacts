# Story format for Ricks-style fact shorts

## What the channel does
- A true story told as a funny animated short, 40-70 s, vertical, simple characters in a rich 3D world.
- A lively, expressive narrator (excited, curious, sarcastic, amused, never laughing constantly) over absurd pictures. The characters keep deadpan faces and react to what happens.
- Word-by-word captions, a new subject every 2-3 s, mostly reached by camera travel instead of cuts, a sound on almost every move, music under the voice.

## Lanes (pick by research; rotate)
| Lane | What it is | Example shapes |
|---|---|---|
| A. Story | A true historical con, heist, mistake or stunt | "In 1925, one man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice." |
| B. Loophole | Someone read the rules and found a gap, usually modern | "He bought thousands of puddings to win a free prize" (check it is true first) |
| C. Why can't | A simple question whose answer has a clean physical or logical reason | "Why time travel to the past breaks the rules" |
| D. Weird modern | Something odd from the news or everyday life this month | Product, law, record or glitch with a visible absurd moment |
| E. How it really works | An everyday thing explained through one funny wrong assumption | "Why the first person to try X was wrong" |

The six beats stay the same in every lane; only the "genius" changes. In lanes B-E the hero can be a confident assumption, a rule, a company or a belief instead of a person: show it being sure, show the clue, then the payoff. Lane C and E need a visual metaphor (a character holding the idea, an object that stands for it): if no picture carries the joke, choose another topic. Modern settings (shop, office, space, street) need their own set and props: put that in the shot plan, built from the code-made props in `templates/bridge/props.ts` plus new builders.

## The six beats ("laugh at a genius")
1. **Hook (0-3 s):** a weird action with no context, one picture. ("In 1925, one man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.")
2. **The laugh:** other characters mock, doubt or ignore the hero. Or an overconfident expert dismisses the clue.
3. **They all look right:** time passes, the hero seems wrong or harmless, the crowd relaxes.
4. **The clue:** one small detail that foreshadows the payoff. Show it, do not explain it.
5. **The payoff:** the event that shows who was right. The visual climax, the one place where physics may be exaggerated.
6. **The science / the reason:** the mechanism in one simple sentence with an everyday analogy, shown as a cutaway or macro view.
- **Callback ending:** the last line twists the first line, and starts an argument or a comment. Show it as a held payoff beat; never end on a walk-away.

## Topic types that work
- A confident person proven wrong by physics, maths or nature.
- A con, a mistake or a stunt that sounds made up but is documented.
- An object or rule with a surprising origin ("why X is shaped like that").
- An absurd official rule or decision with a real reason behind it.
Avoid: tragedies with real victims as the joke, living private people, medical, legal or political claims, hate or stereotypes. The target of the joke is overconfidence and smugness.

## Titles
"Why X ...", "How X ...", "What X really meant", "The man who ...". No spoilers of the payoff.

## Comedy toolbox (use at least three kinds per short)
- **Line gags:** understatement, "Luckily ... / Unluckily ...", a flat number or fact delivered like a weather report.
- **Visual gags:** reaction faces, a wrong-scale prop, a tiny background detail, a running gag character (pigeon, man with a sandwich) that pays off at the end.
- **Sound gags:** record scratch, crowd laugh, a tiny ding, a gulp.
- **Rule of three:** two normal beats, the third breaks the pattern.
- **Dialogue beat (optional, at most 2):** the narrator reads a quoted line of 8 words or fewer in the same voice, slightly acted, the speaker is a camera stop, a 6-10 frame pause, the camera glides or racks focus to the listener, whose face lands 3-6 frames after the last word.

## Reaction faces to schedule
laugh (+crowd laugh), shock (+gasp or scratch), nervous (+gulp), dazed (+ding), pained, smug (smile or grin), guilty (whistle), unimpressed (stern, held 12 frames).

## Pacing numbers
- 3.5-3.7 words per second of speech, plus pauses: 120-150 words for 40-55 s.
- A new subject every 2-3 s. A gag every 4-6 s. A sound on almost every move.
- At most 2 hard cuts per 60 s; everything else is camera flow or a short dissolve.
- 12-16 places and 20+ props for a 60 s short; every place gets its own light mood.
